AI PHOTO-TO-REPORT AUTOMATION SPECIFICATION
Generated: 2026-10-01

1. Principle
------------
The system must not let an AI model "inspect a house" by itself. AI converts uploaded
evidence into a structured, reviewable report draft. A licensed/authorized inspector remains
responsible for deciding whether an issue exists, its severity, recommendation, and whether
the report is published.

Each AI draft must show:
- the source photo(s), voice note, measurement, or checklist response;
- detected component and issue candidate;
- confidence/uncertainty;
- suggested report location and wording;
- inspector decision: accept, edit, reject, or mark "needs site verification";
- AI model/version and time of generation.

2. Existing report-workspace pattern
------------------------------------
The inspected Horizon Report Writing workspace is structured around reusable report items,
not only blank text. It includes:
- Photo loading and gallery controls.
- Required-item/completion checks.
- Templates and database search.
- Report-item repetition, customization, and creation.
- Item/section hide-show controls.
- Preview and a separate report publishing/generation/sending action.
- Work-order and mobile-log navigation.

The visible template item controls include component-specific choices and standardized
material/condition wording. For example, the currently visible roof-related template covers
roof direction/orientation, roof-covering material, roof membrane material, flashing
material, roof-slope descriptors, age ranges, repeated items, and blank custom items.

The replacement must retain this structured template model. AI should fill a structured item
proposal, then derive readable report wording from approved items. It must not replace the
underlying inspection data with one opaque generated paragraph.

3. Recommended inspection taxonomy
----------------------------------
Use company-configurable service templates. A standard home-inspection template should
support at least:

1. Site and exterior
   - grading, drainage, driveway, walkways, decks, porches, railings, stairs, exterior
     cladding, windows, doors, flashing, eaves, gutters, downspouts, vegetation.
2. Roofing
   - roof covering, slopes, valleys, penetrations, flashing, skylights, chimneys, gutters,
     drainage, visible damage, age/material, safety/access limitations.
3. Structure, foundation, and crawlspace
   - foundation walls, slabs, framing, beams, columns, settlement indications, moisture,
     drainage, crawlspace access/ventilation/insulation.
4. Attic, insulation, and ventilation
   - attic access, insulation type/depth, ventilation, roof framing, moisture staining,
     wiring, bathroom fan termination.
5. Electrical
   - service entrance, meter, panel, breakers, grounding/bonding, branch wiring, outlets,
     GFCI/AFCI where applicable, fixtures, visible hazards.
6. Plumbing
   - supply piping, drain/waste/vent piping, fixtures, water heater, visible leaks,
     shutoffs, sump pump, water pressure observations, fuel/gas piping where applicable.
7. Heating, cooling, and ventilation
   - furnace/boiler, heat pump, air conditioner, distribution, thermostats, filters,
     condensate, combustion venting, visible labels and maintenance indicators.
8. Interior
   - walls, ceilings, floors, windows, doors, stairs, handrails, kitchen, bathroom,
     laundry, ventilation, visible moisture/finish concerns.
9. Fireplace and solid-fuel equipment
   - hearth, firebox, chimney visible areas, damper, clearance, visible safety conditions.
10. Appliances
   - installed appliances, visible labels, basic operating observations, connection
     condition, and limitations.
11. Safety and limitations
   - inaccessible areas, unsafe conditions, missing safety equipment, and scope limits.

Every company must be able to add, rename, reorder, hide, and require categories/items.

4. What the AI does for each uploaded photo
--------------------------------------------
Step 1: Quality gate
- Detect blur, darkness, low resolution, obstruction, overexposure, duplicates, and
  insufficient framing.
- Return "retake photo" guidance before attempting a finding.

Step 2: Component recognition
- Detect likely component(s), such as roof covering, electrical panel, outlet, water heater,
  furnace, air conditioner, foundation wall, window, stair/railing, plumbing fixture, or
  attic insulation.
- Suggest a taxonomy category and report item. Do not silently assign it.

Step 3: Visible-condition candidate
- Identify visually apparent cues only: staining, corrosion, cracking, missing cover,
  damaged surface, disconnected duct, water evidence, improper clearance, exposed wiring,
  missing handrail, or other configured visual patterns.
- Distinguish "visible indicator" from a diagnosis. For example: "possible moisture staining
  visible" is valid; "active roof leak" requires inspector confirmation/evidence.

Step 4: Evidence marker
- Store normalized bounding boxes/polygons for the detected component or concern.
- Display a marker on the image, with a stable marker ID and an inspector-editable label.
- Support multiple markers per photo and many photos per observation.

Step 5: Draft item
- Create a proposed observation record:
  category, component, location, visible indicator, suggested classification, proposed
  standardized wording, evidence IDs, confidence, and "requires human review" status.
- If the model lacks sufficient evidence, create a missing-information task rather than a
  finding.

5. Mobile UI behavior
---------------------
After photo capture, show:

Photo
  -> AI suggests: "Electrical > Main panel"
  -> Marker: "Possible unprotected opening"
  -> Buttons: Accept category | Change category | Add note | Retake | Ignore AI
  -> Draft: "An opening was visible at the electrical panel. Recommend evaluation and repair
     by a qualified electrician."
  -> Buttons: Accept draft | Edit | Reject | Needs verification

The inspector must be able to:
- draw, move, resize, hide, or delete AI markers;
- choose the component/category manually;
- dictate or type an alternative note;
- link the photo to an existing report item;
- mark the photo as contextual/not a defect;
- work fully offline;
- see upload, processing, AI-draft, review, and sync states.

6. Report-generation pipeline
-----------------------------
1. Upload photo and create immutable MediaAsset with checksum, capture time, device ID, and
   optional sanitized location metadata.
2. Create thumbnails and run malware scanning.
3. Send a minimized, tenant-scoped image/context package to the vision/OCR pipeline.
4. Write AI results as AISuggestion records; do not modify the report draft directly.
5. Inspector accepts/edits/rejects suggestions.
6. Accepted suggestions create or update ReportItem/Observation records with EvidenceLink
   records.
7. Rules engine performs deterministic validation:
   required categories, required photos, unsupported recommendation, missing location,
   missing severity, duplicate observation, and contradictory limitation/finding.
8. Text-generation service renders only approved structured observations into the selected
   company template.
9. Inspector/reviewer runs Preview.
10. Authorized reviewer validates and publishes an immutable report revision.

7. Models and technical approach
--------------------------------
Use a pipeline, not one general chat prompt:

- Vision quality model: blur, brightness, duplicate/near-duplicate, framing.
- Component detector/segmenter: identifies major building systems and produces bounding
  boxes/masks.
- OCR model: labels, serial/model numbers, electrical-panel markings, equipment data plates.
- Multimodal model: converts inspector notes plus approved detected evidence into a proposed
  observation; it must be constrained by the template schema.
- Rules engine: deterministic company/jurisdiction requirements and report completeness.
- Retrieval layer: company-approved template language, disclaimers, classification wording,
  and service-specific guidance. Never retrieve another tenant's data.
- Human review layer: the only source of accepted observations and final publication.

Prefer JSON-schema outputs from the AI service, for example:

{
  "category": "electrical",
  "component": "service panel",
  "location": "garage",
  "visible_indicators": ["unprotected opening"],
  "suggested_classification": "repair",
  "evidence": [{"media_asset_id": "photo-uuid", "marker_id": "marker-uuid"}],
  "draft_wording": "An opening was visible at the service panel.",
  "recommendation": "Have a qualified electrician evaluate and repair as needed.",
  "confidence": 0.71,
  "requires_human_review": true
}

Do not allow an AI response to create a published report, invoice, appointment, or external
message directly.

8. Automation list
------------------
Safe to automate after configuration:
- Media checksum, duplicate detection, thumbnail generation, malware scan, upload retry.
- Photo-quality feedback.
- Component/category suggestions.
- OCR extraction into a reviewable structured field.
- Caption and draft-observation suggestions.
- Report-section assignment suggestions.
- Missing-photo/missing-item/completeness warnings.
- Duplicate/contradictory-observation warnings.
- Approved-template wording formatting.
- Draft summary and draft client-email text.
- Scheduling duration/travel suggestions and reminder drafts.

Must require a human decision:
- Accepting a defect/condition conclusion.
- Selecting severity and recommendation.
- Diagnosing a system from uncertain or incomplete evidence.
- Changing a work-order status to complete.
- Approving a report.
- Publishing, sending, or sharing a report.
- Charging a payment method, issuing a refund, exporting data, or deleting records.

9. Training, evaluation, and rollout
-------------------------------------
- Start with 3-5 high-volume categories such as roofing, electrical panels, HVAC equipment,
  water heaters, and exterior/interior damage—not every component at once.
- Build a consented, de-identified evaluation set with inspector-reviewed labels,
  components, marker locations, and final wording.
- Measure component precision/recall, photo-quality accuracy, marker overlap, missing-item
  recall, AI acceptance/edit/rejection rate, and false-positive safety findings.
- Release in shadow mode first: AI produces suggestions visible to reviewers but cannot alter
  report records.
- Pilot with selected inspectors, then expand only when measured accuracy and review burden
  meet agreed thresholds.
- Retain a manual-only workflow for every feature and permit company-level AI opt-out.

10. Safety, legal, and privacy requirements
--------------------------------------------
- Explain to inspectors that AI suggestions require professional review.
- Show uncertainty and missing-evidence states rather than inventing a conclusion.
- Keep original media, generated marker, prompt version, AI result, human changes, and final
  wording in a traceable audit chain.
- Redact or minimize client names, addresses, payment data, and unrelated report content
  before AI processing.
- Use tenant isolation, encrypted storage/transit, provider retention controls, and
  no-training-on-customer-data contractual terms.
- Perform jurisdiction-specific legal/compliance review before any automated recommendation
  wording is released.
