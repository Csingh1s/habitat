1. Scope and evidence
---------------------
This document maps the home-inspection systems visible in the authenticated Horizon report
workspace to safe AI assistance and mandatory human review.

Verified Horizon report categories:
1. Roofing
2. Exterior
3. Structure
4. Electrical
5. Heating
6. Cooling
7. Insulation
8. Plumbing
9. Interior

Verified report classifications:
- Description
- Limitations
- Recommendations

Verified Roofing template concepts:
- home orientation;
- sloped/flat roofing material;
- sloped/flat flashing material;
- roof shape;
- probability of leakage;
- approximate age and typical life expectancy;
- private notes;
- controlled materials such as asphalt/composition/wood shingles, concrete/clay tile,
  fiber cement, slate, metal, roll roofing, modified bitumen, and built-up membrane.

The shared report's remaining category tabs require server-side inspection context that is not
preserved by direct read-only navigation. This document therefore uses the verified nine-system
taxonomy and clearly separates: (a) observed Horizon structure, and (b) proposed implementation
coverage. It does not reveal any client report content, property address, photo, or finding.

2. Non-negotiable accuracy statement
------------------------------------
DO NOT promise "perfect accuracy."

There is no valid universal accuracy rate for home-inspection AI. Accuracy changes by:
- system category;
- photo quality, light, angle, and distance;
- house age, construction type, weather, and equipment;
- model/version;
- whether the task is object recognition, OCR, visible-cue detection, or diagnosis;
- the definition of a true issue;
- inspector labels and reviewer agreement.

The production product must state:
"AI assists with evidence organization and draft wording. A qualified inspector reviews and
approves all findings before publication."

The product must never state:
"AI accurately diagnoses every home defect" or "AI produces a complete inspection report
without human review."

3. Automation levels
--------------------
Level 1: deterministic software
- No model judgment. Use rules, image processing, checksums, and validation.

Level 2: AI suggestion
- AI suggests a component/category/caption/OCR value. Inspector can correct it.

Level 3: AI review candidate
- AI identifies a visible cue and marks it in a photo. Inspector decides whether it is a
  concern, defect, limitation, or irrelevant.

Level 4: human-only conclusion
- Qualified inspector decides functional condition, cause, safety, code relevance, severity,
  recommendation, and final report content.

4. System-by-system AI coverage
--------------------------------

4.1 Roofing
Observed Horizon scope:
- roof shape, orientation, sloped/flat materials, flashing, approximate age/life expectancy,
  leakage probability, limitations, and recommendations.

AI can assist well (Level 2):
- photo quality and duplicate detection;
- distinguish roof surface, flashing, chimney, skylight, gutter, downspout, valley, vent,
  and roof-edge context;
- suggest visible covering material;
- suggest orientation when phone direction/context is reliable;
- OCR labels where present;
- draft neutral photo captions;
- identify possible visible missing/damaged surface material, debris, or discoloration;
- flag missing required roof views according to the company checklist.

AI can flag only (Level 3):
- possible missing/damaged shingle or tile;
- possible exposed fastener;
- possible deteriorated flashing;
- possible ponding/debris/wear cue;
- possible staining or roof-surface irregularity.

Inspector must decide (Level 4):
- whether the roof leaks;
- whether deterioration is active/material;
- remaining life expectancy;
- whether repair or replacement is needed;
- source of moisture;
- access limitations and safety;
- final probability of leakage and recommendation.

4.2 Exterior
Proposed scope:
- site drainage, grading, cladding/siding, trim, windows, doors, decks, porches, stairs,
  guards/handrails, driveways/walkways, gutters/downspouts, vegetation, and visible flashing.

AI can assist well:
- identify common exterior component categories;
- classify photo as siding/window/door/deck/stair/gutter/driveway;
- detect obvious missing handrail candidate, visible surface damage, paint deterioration,
  staining, or obstruction candidate;
- suggest material/caption and required-photo coverage.

Inspector must decide:
- water-management effectiveness and grade/drainage condition;
- rot extent, weatherproofing, structural attachment, window/door operation;
- safety/code relevance of railings/guards;
- cause and significance of cracking/damage;
- recommendation.

4.3 Structure
Proposed scope:
- foundation walls, slabs, framing, beams, columns, crawlspace, settlement signs, visible
  moisture, and structural access limitations.

AI can assist well:
- identify foundation wall, slab, beam, column, framing, crawlspace, and insulation context;
- locate/mark a visible crack, stain, spall, or surface irregularity;
- organize evidence photos by area.

AI can flag only:
- possible crack/linear feature;
- possible moisture staining;
- possible surface deterioration;
- possible unsupported/altered framing cue.

Inspector must decide:
- structural significance;
- active movement versus cosmetic cracking;
- moisture source;
- whether specialist evaluation is needed;
- safety and repair recommendation.

Structure is a high-risk category. No AI visual cue may become a defect or safety conclusion
without inspector review.

4.4 Electrical
Proposed scope:
- service entrance, meter, panel, breakers, labeling, grounding/bonding, branch wiring,
  outlets, switches, fixtures, GFCI/AFCI checks, and visible hazards.

AI can assist well:
- identify panel/outlet/switch/meter/fixture images;
- OCR clear panel labels, manufacturer/model markings, and visible text;
- detect possible missing cover, open knockout, exposed conductor, corrosion, or labeling
  irregularity;
- organize photos by electrical item.

Inspector must decide:
- electrical safety;
- whether a condition is code-relevant or defective;
- panel capacity, grounding/bonding correctness, GFCI/AFCI requirements;
- whether circuits function;
- recommendation and urgency.

Electrical requires human review for every AI-visible-cue flag.

4.5 Heating
Proposed scope:
- furnace, boiler, heat pump, distribution, thermostat, filter, condensate, combustion
  venting, fuel connection, labels, visible condition, and functional observations.

AI can assist well:
- identify major equipment category;
- OCR model/serial/manufacturer labels;
- suggest photo caption and report section;
- flag visible corrosion, disconnected duct/vent candidate, missing filter candidate, or
  visible condensate issue candidate.

Inspector must decide:
- operation/performance;
- combustion safety;
- venting compliance;
- maintenance, repair, replacement, and life-expectancy conclusion;
- diagnostic tests and recommendation.

4.6 Cooling
Proposed scope:
- condenser, evaporator/air handler, refrigerant-line insulation, disconnect, condensate,
  thermostat, distribution, labels, and operation.

AI can assist well:
- identify condenser/air-handler/thermostat;
- OCR labels;
- detect obvious exterior damage, vegetation obstruction candidate, missing line insulation
  candidate, or visible corrosion;
- capture required equipment photos.

Inspector must decide:
- cooling operation, refrigerant condition, capacity, electrical safety, airflow, leakage,
  and repair/replacement recommendation.

4.7 Insulation and ventilation
Proposed scope:
- attic/crawlspace access, insulation type/depth, ventilation, roof framing, moisture
  staining, wiring, and bathroom-fan termination.

AI can assist well:
- identify attic, insulation, exposed framing, vent, and visible wiring;
- suggest insulation category (for example batt/loose-fill) when image quality is sufficient;
- flag visible dark staining, disconnected duct candidate, or insulation coverage gaps;
- identify missing expected attic/insulation photos.

Inspector must decide:
- insulation R-value/depth accuracy;
- adequacy of ventilation;
- moisture source;
- air-sealing quality;
- unsafe wiring or inaccessible-area conclusion.

4.8 Plumbing
Proposed scope:
- supply/drain/waste/vent piping, fixtures, water heater, shutoffs, sump pump, visible leaks,
  fuel/gas piping where applicable, and functional observations.

AI can assist well:
- identify sink, toilet, faucet, pipe, water heater, valve, sump pump, and visible labels;
- OCR equipment labels;
- flag possible corrosion, moisture staining, drip, or missing connection candidate;
- route photos to plumbing/water-heater report item.

Inspector must decide:
- whether moisture indicates an active leak;
- water pressure, drainage performance, shutoff operation, venting, fuel/gas safety;
- hidden-piping condition;
- repair scope/urgency and recommendation.

4.9 Interior
Proposed scope:
- walls, ceilings, floors, windows, doors, stairs, railings, kitchens, bathrooms, laundry,
  installed appliances, ventilation, and visible finish/moisture concerns.

AI can assist well:
- classify room/component;
- identify appliance/window/door/stair/railing/sink/toilet/cabinet;
- caption photos;
- flag a visible crack, stain, missing handrail candidate, damaged finish, or accessible
  appliance label;
- suggest report section/item and identify missing room coverage.

Inspector must decide:
- functional operation of windows/doors/appliances;
- source/significance of stains/cracks;
- water intrusion;
- safety/egress/accessibility/code implications;
- recommendation.

5. Accuracy measurement by task
--------------------------------
Measure task performance separately. Never combine these into one "home inspection accuracy"
number.

| Task | Measure | Example decision |
|---|---|---|
| Photo quality | precision, recall, false reject rate | ask user to retake or proceed |
| Duplicate photo | precision/recall of duplicate pairs | flag only; inspector can retain |
| Category routing | top-1 and top-3 accuracy | suggest report section |
| Component recognition | precision/recall by component | suggest panel, water heater, roof, etc. |
| OCR | field/character accuracy | editable label value |
| Visual marker | IoU/overlap with inspector-marked region | display review marker |
| Visible-cue flag | precision, recall, false-positive rate, missed-cue rate | create review candidate only |
| Draft wording | inspector accept/edit/reject rate | show editable wording |
| Completeness warning | true warning/false warning rate | warn, never auto-block without rule |
| Summary | reviewer correction rate | editable summary only |

Formula definitions:
- Precision = true positives / all positive AI flags.
- Recall = true positives / all issues labeled in the evaluation set.
- False-positive rate = non-issues flagged / all non-issues.
- Missed-cue rate = labeled visible cues not flagged / all labeled visible cues.
- Acceptance rate = accepted unchanged / all AI suggestions.

6. Accuracy program before production
--------------------------------------
1. Build a consented, de-identified, inspector-reviewed photo dataset.
2. Label every photo with:
   - category/component;
   - whether a visible cue exists;
   - exact marker/region;
   - final inspector interpretation;
   - limitations and confidence;
   - approved report wording where applicable.
3. Split data by property, not photo, into training/tuning, validation, and held-out test
   sets. Photos from the same home must not appear in both training and test sets.
4. Test separately for Roofing, Exterior, Structure, Electrical, Heating, Cooling,
   Insulation, Plumbing, and Interior.
5. Test different lighting, device cameras, seasons, home ages, materials, distances, and
   difficult/obstructed views.
6. Compare models and prompts using the held-out set before any production launch.
7. Run shadow mode: AI creates suggestions, but cannot modify report records. Inspectors
   review and label the quality.
8. Run a limited pilot with audit review every week.
9. Release individual tasks/categories only when product, quality, and safety owners approve
   the measured results.
10. Continuously monitor drift, false positives, missed cues, edit/reject rate, published
    report amendments, and client/inspector complaints.

7. Release rules by risk
-----------------------
Low-risk tasks may launch after measured quality review:
- photo quality;
- duplicate detection;
- OCR as editable draft;
- category/component suggestion;
- captions;
- draft wording from inspector-approved structured data.

Medium-risk tasks must launch in shadow mode and remain inspector-reviewed:
- photo markers;
- visible-cue flags;
- missing-photo/completeness warnings;
- report-item suggestions.

High-risk tasks must never be automatic:
- structural, electrical, plumbing, roofing, heating/cooling, safety, or code conclusions;
- severity and recommendation;
- report approval/publishing/delivery.

8. Production client-delivery controls
--------------------------------------
Before a client receives a report, enforce:
- all required items completed or waived with inspector reason;
- all photo uploads/media processing completed;
- all "needs verification" observations resolved;
- no unresolved AI/model/system error;
- inspector review of every accepted AI-assisted item;
- report preview generated from the exact publish revision;
- qualified inspector approval;
- optional second quality reviewer based on company policy/risk;
- explicit publish confirmation;
- explicit recipient/delivery confirmation;
- immutable audit record of evidence, AI suggestion, human decision, publish revision, and
  delivery event.

9. Builder acceptance criteria
------------------------------
1. Model output is always a draft and cannot directly create a published finding.
2. Every AI-assisted observation retains source-photo IDs and editable markers.
3. The inspector can reject all AI output and complete the report manually.
4. The API blocks publication unless an authorized human approves the exact report revision.
5. The API blocks client delivery unless the report is published and delivery is separately
   confirmed.
6. Accuracy dashboards show metrics by system category and task, not one overall percentage.
7. The product records model/version, prompt/template version, source evidence, AI output,
   inspector action, final wording, and post-publication amendments.
8. Any model/category can be disabled immediately without preventing manual inspection or
   report completion.
